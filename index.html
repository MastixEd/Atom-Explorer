What is an Atom?

Everything around you — your desk, the air, your body — is made of tiny building blocks called atoms.

An atom is like a tiny solar system:

  • In the middle is the nucleus (the sun).
  • Around it zoom tiny electrons (like planets).

The nucleus is made of two kinds of particles:

  • Protons — positive charge (like + magnets)
  • Neutrons — no charge (neutral helpers)

Electrons are negative. Positive and negative attract, so electrons stay near the nucleus.

Real electrons don’t travel on neat tracks like planets — they form a fuzzy “cloud”. This model is a simplified picture and not to scale: the real nucleus is about 100,000 times smaller than the whole atom!

Atomic Structure (Bohr Model + Modern Notes)

An atom consists of a dense, positively charged nucleus surrounded by a cloud of negatively charged electrons. The nucleus is only about 10⁻⁵ of the atom’s diameter; the rest of the volume is where the electron cloud has non-zero probability density.

The nucleus contains:

  • Protons (charge +1e, mass ≈ 1.6726 × 10⁻²⁷ kg)
  • Neutrons (charge 0, mass ≈ 1.6749 × 10⁻²⁷ kg)

Electrons (charge −1e, mass ≈ 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg ≈ 1/1836 of a proton) occupy discrete energy levels (shells).

Atomic number (Z) = number of protons → defines the element.
Mass number (A) = protons + neutrons.
In a neutral atom, electrons = protons.

Note: The planetary model is useful for visualization and is not to scale. In quantum mechanics, electrons exist as probability clouds (orbitals), not fixed paths.

Subatomic Particles

Proton

Positive charge (+1). Lives in the nucleus. The number of protons tells us what element the atom is (1 = hydrogen, 6 = carbon, 8 = oxygen…).

Made of three even smaller pieces called quarks: two “Up” quarks and one “Down” quark. These quarks are glued tightly together by special particles called gluons.

Charge: +1 e. Rest mass: 938.272 MeV/c². Quark content: uud (two up quarks of charge +2/3 each + one down quark of charge −1/3). Bound by the strong force mediated by gluons. Determines chemical identity (atomic number Z). Note: the quark rest masses account for only ≈1% of the proton’s mass; the rest comes from the energy of the gluon field and quark motion (E = mc²).

Neutron

No charge (neutral). Also lives in the nucleus. Helps hold the protons together so they don’t push each other away. Adds mass but does not change the element. Made of one Up quark and two Down quarks, also held by gluons.

Charge: 0. Rest mass: 939.565 MeV/c². Quark content: udd (one up + two down). Stabilizes the nucleus against electrostatic repulsion of protons via the residual strong force. Free neutrons decay via β⁻ with mean lifetime ≈ 880 s.

Electron

Negative charge (−1). Zooms around the nucleus in shells (energy levels). Almost no mass compared to protons/neutrons. Responsible for chemistry — atoms share or trade electrons to form molecules.

Charge: −1 e. Rest mass: 0.511 MeV/c². Fundamental lepton (not made of quarks). Occupies atomic orbitals; arrangement determines chemical bonding, spectra, and reactivity. In the Bohr model they occupy circular orbits; in QM they form standing-wave probability distributions.

Quarks, Flavours & Gluons

Protons and neutrons are not the smallest pieces! They are made of even tinier particles called quarks.

There are 6 different “flavours” (types) of quarks:

  • Up and Down – the everyday ones that make protons & neutrons
  • Charm and Strange – rarer, heavier
  • Top and Bottom – the heaviest and rarest

How a proton is made:
2 Up quarks + 1 Down quark = Proton
(Up has charge +2/3, Down has −1/3 → total charge +1)

How a neutron is made:
1 Up quark + 2 Down quarks = Neutron
(total charge 0)

The quarks are stuck together by super-strong “glue” particles called gluons. Gluons carry the strong force inside each proton and neutron, and a leftover of that glue also holds the whole nucleus together.

Quarks are elementary fermions that combine to form hadrons. There are six flavours, organised in three generations:

  • 1st generation: up (u, +2/3 e), down (d, −1/3 e)
  • 2nd generation: charm (c, +2/3 e), strange (s, −1/3 e)
  • 3rd generation: top (t, +2/3 e), bottom (b, −1/3 e)

Proton composition: uud
Charge: (+2/3) + (+2/3) + (−1/3) = +1
Baryon number = 1

Neutron composition: udd
Charge: (+2/3) + (−1/3) + (−1/3) = 0

Gluons are the gauge bosons of the strong interaction (QCD). They carry colour charge (unlike photons) and can interact with each other. There are 8 types of gluon. The strong force confines quarks inside hadrons (colour confinement) and binds protons and neutrons in the nucleus (residual strong force via meson exchange).

Free quarks are never observed; they are always confined in colour-neutral combinations (baryons or mesons).

How are they formed?

Right after the Big Bang, the universe was extremely hot. As it cooled:

  • Tiny quarks joined (with the help of gluons) to make protons and neutrons.
  • Protons and neutrons stuck together into light nuclei (mostly hydrogen and helium).
  • Later, electrons joined the nuclei to make the first atoms.

Heavier atoms (carbon, oxygen, iron…) are made inside stars and during supernova explosions!

Quark–hadron transition (≈10 microseconds after the Big Bang): as the quark–gluon plasma cooled below about 2 × 10¹² K, quarks and gluons became confined into hadrons (protons and neutrons).

Big Bang nucleosynthesis (≈3–20 minutes): protons and neutrons fused into deuterium, helium-3, helium-4 and traces of lithium-7 (and beryllium-7, which decays to Li-7).

Stellar nucleosynthesis: hydrogen fusion → helium → carbon, oxygen, neon… up to the iron peak via successive fusion stages (fusion beyond iron absorbs energy rather than releasing it).

Elements heavier than iron are built by neutron capture: the slow s-process in aging (AGB) stars, and the rapid r-process in supernovae and neutron-star mergers.

Electrons were captured by nuclei as the universe cooled (~380 000 years after Big Bang → recombination era → first neutral atoms).
